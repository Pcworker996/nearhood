# Community Information

Shared language for understanding neighborhood activities and conditions through community observations.

## Language

**Report**:
A contributor's original submitted account, based on personal observation or information from another source. It preserves the contributor's words, provenance, location, submission time, and reported observation time. It can contain multiple observations and remains inspectable as the shared account changes.
_Avoid_: Post

**Observation**:
A structured interpretation of what a contributor claims in a report, including the subject, state, observation time, and relevant spatial scope. It traces back to the original report and is distinct from the contributor's submitted words; interpretation alone does not establish accuracy.

**Firsthand report**:
A report the contributor identifies as based on their own observation. That declaration identifies its origin but does not establish its accuracy.

**Source-based report**:
A report the contributor identifies as information from another source. Multiple reports referring to the same source do not by themselves provide independent corroboration.

**Observation time**:
The time the reported situation was observed, which may differ from when the report was submitted. It places the account in the happening's chronology and can be unknown.

**Submission time**:
The time a contributor submits a report or report update. It does not establish when the described situation was observed.

**Happening**:
An evolving real-world situation that observations refer to. For a disruption or safety concern, it is one problem at one place or object, followed over time: it may stop, may be reported resolved, and may recur. For an activity, it is one scheduled instance. Multiple reports may describe it, and its shared account changes as evidence arrives. Reports about the same problem at the same place belong to the same happening unless evidence shows a different situation, such as a different problem, an explicitly new object or event, or a different activity instance. Unknown history between observations does not by itself separate happenings, and sharing a category or place does not by itself establish that reports refer to the same happening.
_Avoid_: Label

**Category**:
A broad kind of happening, such as an activity, disruption, or safety concern. A category describes the kind of situation, not its identity.
_Avoid_: Label

**Matching**:
Determining whether a report refers to an existing happening or a new happening.

**Report stack**:
The collection of attributed reports associated with a happening that readers can inspect. It includes differing perspectives, biased accounts, and conflicting observations.

**Shared summary**:
A concise readable presentation of a happening's shared account. Key statements reference supporting observations and their original reports, while the full report stack remains available for inspection.

**Shared account**:
The provisional current interpretation of what a happening's available evidence supports, including its chronology, spatial scope, changes, disagreement, and uncertainty. It may change as new observations arrive and is not itself independent evidence.

**Spatial scope**:
The part of a place or situation that an observation describes, such as one lane or entrance. Different states in different parts do not by themselves establish contradictory accounts or full resolution.

**Resolution report**:
A report that a previously reported disruption or safety concern may have ended. It is new information about the happening, but does not by itself establish that the happening is resolved.

**Recurrence**:
A later report of a disruption or safety concern after it stopped, may have stopped, or was reported resolved. It belongs to the same happening unless evidence shows a different situation. Whether the condition was repaired in between can remain unknown; the shared account preserves that gap and does not claim the condition was continuous across it.

**Reported resolved**:
A provisional status indicating that sufficient evidence supports the end of the original disruption or safety concern, including its full reported extent. It expresses the supported community account rather than a definitive finding of resolution.

**Resolution assessment**:
An assessment of whether reports sufficiently support the end of the original condition, considering the same situation, full extent, observation timing, source support, and remaining conflicts. Its evidence explanation identifies supporting reports and unresolved uncertainty.

**Partial improvement**:
A change that reduces the extent or impact of a disruption or safety concern without establishing that the original condition has fully ended.

**No recent updates**:
A freshness indicator that the available reports are too old to establish current conditions. It does not establish that a happening remains active or has resolved.

**Past activity**:
A scheduled activity whose scheduled end time has passed. This identifies its place in the schedule without establishing that it actually occurred.

**Activity organizer**:
The designated contributor responsible for an activity's schedule and cancellation status, explicitly choosing that role when creating the activity. First reporting or sharing someone else's activity does not establish organizer status.

**Interested**:
A person's private choice to receive updates about an activity. It expresses interest without committing to attend and does not create a public list or count.

**Follow updates**:
A person's private choice to receive in-app updates about provisional status changes to a disruption or safety concern. It does not create a public follower list or count.

**RSVP**:
A person's response indicating whether they intend to attend an activity. It is distinct from choosing to receive updates.

**Discovery**:
Finding relevant happenings already stored in the app in response to a reader's request. Expanding the search area broadens the geographic search of stored happenings.

**Source check**:
An assessment of relevant information outside the app, with dated, linked findings describing agreement, conflicts, and what remains unknown.

**Reports received**:
A provisional status acknowledging available reports without establishing resolution. It can describe a happening whose current observations conflict.

**Report update**:
A contributor's later observation or correction attached to their earlier report. The earlier account and its time remain part of the report's history.

**New observation**:
A report update describing what the contributor observed at a later time. A changed condition does not make the earlier observation inaccurate.

**Correction**:
A report update identifying an inaccuracy in the contributor's earlier account, such as the wrong location. It corrects the earlier account rather than describing a later change in conditions.

**Supporting evidence**:
Material a contributor submits to support a report or report update. Its relevance and limitations affect how much support it provides; submission alone does not establish accuracy.
